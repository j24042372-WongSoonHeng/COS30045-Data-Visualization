Television Energy Consumption

Understand the energy demands of modern televisions in Australia. Compare technologies like OLED, LED, and LCD to make an informed, energy-efficient choice for your home.

Technology Breakdown

◒

LED

The standard for decades. Utilizes a backlight to illuminate pixels. Generally the most energy-efficient option for everyday viewing.

455 kWh/year
⚡

LCD

An older backlit display technology, now largely phased out in favour of LED backlighting. Less energy-efficient than modern LED panels due to older backlight and diffuser designs.

334 kWh/year
◑

OLED

Self-lit pixels provide perfect blacks. Highly efficient in dark scenes, but can draw significant power when displaying bright, full-screen imagery.

Energy Saving Tips

☼

Adjust Brightness & Picture Modes

Lowering the backlight settings or switching to 'Eco' or 'Standard' mode instead of 'Vivid' can reduce energy consumption by up to 30% without significantly impacting viewing quality in a dim room.

⏻

Kill Standby Power

Smart TVs consume power even when off. Use a smart plug or switch off at the wall to save 'vampire' energy.

📏

Size Matters

A 75-inch TV consumes significantly more power than a 55-inch model of the same technology. Choose a size appropriate for your room to balance immersion with efficiency.

⏱

Enable Auto-Power Off

Ensure sleep timers and auto-power down settings are active. Falling asleep with the TV on is a major source of wasted energy in Australian households.
